Project

CFD-guided design of a thermal-fluid subsystem

Using CFD and experiments to improve pressure loss and temperature distribution in a thermal-fluid component.

Role: Modeling & validation · Context: Thermal-fluid system development
CFD Heat transfer ANSYS Fluent / OpenFOAM MATLAB
← Back to project list

Overview

The aim of this project was to evaluate and improve a thermal-fluid subsystem where flow distribution and temperature uniformity were critical to performance and reliability. Physical testing alone would have been expensive and time-consuming, so CFD was used as a primary design tool, with targeted experiments providing validation.

I set up three-dimensional CFD models, applied appropriate boundary conditions, and iterated on geometry to reduce losses and hotspots. Experimental measurements were used to calibrate the models and build confidence in the predicted trends.

Methods & tools

The numerical work focused on replicating the key physics of the subsystem while keeping models efficient enough for design iteration.

  • Geometry cleanup and mesh generation
  • Steady and transient simulations as needed
  • Grid independence checks and model verification
  • Post-processing of velocity, pressure, and temperature fields

MATLAB/Python scripts supported batch runs and automated extraction of performance metrics such as pressure drop and outlet temperature distributions.

Experimental validation

To ground the CFD results, I participated in planning and analyzing experiments using an instrumented test setup. Measured flow rates, pressures, and temperatures were compared directly against simulation predictions.

Differences between simulation and experiment were used to refine boundary conditions, material properties, and, where justified, aspects of the turbulence or heat transfer modeling approach.

Results & impact

The project identified design changes that reduced pressure loss while improving temperature uniformity. The validated CFD workflow now serves as a template for similar components, allowing future designs to be evaluated earlier and more efficiently.

The work also clarified the limits of the modeling assumptions, helping stakeholders understand when further experiments are necessary and when simulation alone is sufficient.
