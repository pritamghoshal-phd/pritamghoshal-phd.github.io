A structure that computes.

I work on mechanical systems that carry out computation in their own physics — no processor, very little power. Bistability supplies the nonlinearity, viscoelasticity supplies the memory, and the trade-off between the two decides whether the material can compute at all.

Advised by Prof. James M. Gibert, with Prof. Anil K. Bajaj. Six papers, one of them in Nature Communications; one patent pending; Instructor of Record for a 100-student dynamics course.

Almost everything I have published reduces to three things.

A system that bends the rules, a memory that fades, and a trade-off between the two. The objects change — a truss, a chain of oscillators, a cellular metamaterial, a circuit board, the sound organ of a cicada — but the question underneath does not.

Bistability

Two stable states, one restless system. Push a von Mises truss hard enough and it snaps between them: a strong, cheap source of nonlinearity, and one a structure can supply for itself.

Ü + ζU̇ + U³ − 3U² + (λ+2)U = γ cos ΩT

Fading memory

An electrical reservoir gets short-term memory from a delay line. A mechanical one has only viscoelasticity: the state depends on the history of deformation, not on the deformation now.

(1/(1−β)) dU/dT + U = dΣ/dT + Σ

The trade-off

Memory and nonlinearity pull against each other. Tune one up and the other collapses. Finding where they balance — set by the Deborah number — is the whole problem.

De = λ / t_obs

I teach the course I would have wanted.

This autumn I am Instructor of Record for ME 274, Basic Mechanics II (Dynamics) — a core undergraduate course of 100 students, with three teaching assistants reporting to me. I set the lectures, the homework, the quizzes and the exams, and I write MATLAB problem sets so that students meet the same system twice: once analytically, once computationally.

The moment I teach for is the one where a student stops rearranging symbols and starts predicting what the system will do. Dynamics is unusually good for this, because the prediction is checkable: the pendulum either swings the way you said or it does not.

I mentor on the same principle. Failure is data, feedback runs in both directions, and the aim is a student who no longer needs me.
