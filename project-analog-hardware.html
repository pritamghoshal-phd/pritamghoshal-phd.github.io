Nonlinear dynamics built in silicon, not simulated

FPAA and PCB systems that physically obey the equations I study — and use that behaviour to classify structural damage in real time, without a processor in the loop.

Role: Circuit design, fabrication and testing · Application: Structural health monitoring

Why build the equation instead of solving it

A numerical solver approximates a differential equation in discrete steps. An analog circuit built from integrators, summers and nonlinear elements does something different: its voltages are the state variables, and they evolve continuously because the physics of the components leaves them no choice. The equation is not being solved — it is being obeyed.

That distinction matters for physical reservoir computing. If the value of a reservoir is that its own dynamics perform the nonlinear transformation for free, then implementing that reservoir on a digital processor gives away the entire advantage. Analog hardware keeps it: microwatts of power, no clock, no sampling, and a response that arrives as fast as the circuit settles.

I used field-programmable analog arrays for rapid iteration — reconfigurable analog blocks that let a topology be changed without re-spinning a board — and moved designs onto custom PCBs once the architecture stabilised.

Figure 1 — An equation, built out of op-amps

Figure 1. A nonlinear oscillator realised directly in analog hardware. Two cascaded integrators produce velocity and displacement from acceleration; a cubic block supplies the nonlinear restoring term, and summing everything back to the input closes the loop. The circuit does not compute the equation step by step — it obeys it, continuously and at the speed of the electronics.

The circuits

The core building block is a chain of integrators with nonlinear feedback, which realises a Duffing-type oscillator directly in hardware.

The practical difficulty is that analog components are imperfect in ways the mathematics is not: offsets drift, gains vary between nominally identical parts, and a nonlinearity intended as cubic is only approximately so. Some of that had to be trimmed out — and some of it, usefully, adds the cell-to-cell variation a reservoir actually benefits from.

Hardware machine learning

With the reservoir in analog, only the readout needs training — and a linear readout is cheap enough to run on a microcontroller, or to implement in analog as well.

For structural health monitoring this yields a sensor node that classifies rather than merely records: instead of streaming raw vibration data for offline analysis, the node reports condition directly.

  • Vibration signal drives the analog reservoir
  • Circuit state provides high-dimensional features
  • Linear readout trained for classification
  • Real-time operation at very low power
Figure 2 — Damage detection on an analog reservoirrunning

Figure 2. Vibration from a structure drives the analog nonlinear circuit. Because the circuit is sensitive to the character of its input, a healthy structure and a damaged one drive it onto visibly different attractors — left, the circuit state; right, the separation between the two classes. Introduce damage and watch the attractor deform and the classifier margin open up. No digital signal processing happens anywhere in this loop.

What it demonstrates

Damage changes a structure's dynamics — stiffness drops, damping shifts, new harmonics appear. Those changes are often subtle in a raw spectrum but pronounced once passed through a sensitive nonlinear system, because the reservoir amplifies differences between inputs that a linear filter would treat as nearly identical.

That is the same property that makes a nonlinear system good for classification and bad for long-range prediction, and it is why the reservoir computing work spends so much effort locating the boundary. Here the goal is not to predict a signal but to separate two classes, so the useful operating point sits closer to the chaotic edge than it would for a forecasting task.

The wider ambition is monitoring hardware that can be left on a structure indefinitely: no processor, negligible power, and an output that says healthy or not rather than producing data somebody has to analyse later.
