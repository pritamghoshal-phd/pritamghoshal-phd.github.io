Teaching & mentoring

Teaching & mentoring philosophy.

How I approach the classroom, and what my mentees can expect from me.

← Back to portfolio

Help my mentees become confident and independent. When something fails, treat it as data rather than failure.

I started mentoring because someone once created space for me to try, stumble, and try again, without ever treating those mistakes like verdicts. That generosity shaped how I work and how I show up for others. My motto is simple: help my mentees become confident and independent. I am a “learn by doing” person. When something fails, I prefer to treat it as data rather than failure. I believe a lot in taking insights from failures and that it helps build objectivity and critical thinking.

I believe that curiosity fuels everything. I love sending a short paper, a quirky dataset, or a tool I just found and asking my mentees what they think about it. These quick exchanges help sharpen their judgment. Over time, mentees find themselves explaining tough ideas simply and clearly and even enjoying the process. I don’t believe anyone is a blank slate; mentees arrive with strengths, history, and instincts worth respecting. I begin by asking what they already know and I scaffold the next phase of the work, so it is challenging enough to grow but at the same time, not so overwhelming for the mentees. When mentees can connect new knowledge to things they already know, it feels like they made progress.

I care about both the work and the person doing the work. Professionally, I help my mentees identify the core skills to succeed in their career path and devise a definite roadmap to learn lab-based skills like laser vibrometry and silicone molding. Personally, I help my mentees build sustainable routines and grow confidence through small, visible wins. I also practice enabling my mentees to collaborate with others, encourage them to attend and participate actively in group meetings, give them specific feedback, and share credit where it’s due. I believe that a successful mentoring relationship relies heavily on active feedback, and this runs in both ways. My feedback is timely, specific, and grounded in examples. I highlight what’s working, point out what’s unclear, and suggest concrete next steps. I ask for the same from my mentees. I prefer asking my mentees what helps and what doesn’t or how they prefer to learn. I think that mentoring is not a one-size-fits-all kind of recipe and hence I am very adaptable to my mentees’ needs.

I am a big advocate of inclusivity and fairness. I take every measure to ensure my mentees feel respected and not intimidated to ask “basic” questions or share half-formed ideas. From me, my mentees can expect preparation, presence, and advocacy. I respond promptly, set clear standards, give honest timelines, and help them access the resources and networks they need. From my mentees, I expect ownership of their learning, follow-through on commitments, willingness to test ideas in the open, and transparent communication when plans change. Success to me is more than finished projects. It’s leaving our time together with the ability to frame meaningful questions, design and run sound experiments, communicate results with clarity, collaborate with confidence, and choose the next step with intention. In short, my goal is that my mentees leave our time together more curious, more capable, and confident enough to chart their own path.

In practice

What that looks like day to day.

Teaching

As a teaching assistant I have supported core mechanical engineering courses in dynamics and mechanics — leading problem sessions, holding office hours, and giving feedback on assignments and exams.

I break complex problems into smaller steps and build intuition on simple examples before moving to formal derivations and the general case.

Mentoring

I help mentees identify the core skills for their career path and build a concrete roadmap for lab-based techniques like laser vibrometry and silicone molding.

Alongside that: sustainable routines, confidence through small visible wins, active participation in group meetings, and credit shared where it is due.

Feedback

Timely, specific, grounded in examples. I highlight what is working, name what is unclear, and suggest concrete next steps — and I ask for exactly the same in return.

Mentoring is not one-size-fits-all, so I ask directly what helps and what does not.

Inclusivity

I take every measure to ensure mentees feel respected and never intimidated about asking a "basic" question or sharing a half-formed idea.

From me they can expect preparation, presence and advocacy: prompt replies, clear standards, honest timelines, and access to the resources and networks they need.
