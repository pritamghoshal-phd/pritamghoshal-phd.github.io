Project

Experimental characterization of vibration behavior

Designing and analyzing vibration experiments to quantify dynamic behavior and identify model parameters.

Role: Test design & analysis · Context: Structural dynamics and validation
Vibration testing System identification DAQ MATLAB / Python

Overview

The goal of this project was to experimentally characterize the vibration response of a mechanical assembly and use the data to inform and validate models. This required designing a test setup that could produce controlled excitation, while capturing clean measurements of the system's response.

I worked through the full workflow: from fixture design and sensor placement, to data acquisition, signal processing, and comparison with theoretical predictions.

Experimental setup

I contributed to the design of the test configuration, including:

  • Selecting excitation type (swept-sine, random, or impact)
  • Choosing sensor locations and mounting strategies
  • Configuring the data acquisition system and sampling parameters

Careful attention was paid to avoiding unwanted boundary effects and ensuring that the measured response reflected the behavior of interest.

Analysis & identification

After collecting data, I performed time- and frequency-domain analysis to extract key features such as natural frequencies, damping ratios, and mode shapes.

System identification techniques were used to fit simplified models to the data, which were then compared with analytical and numerical predictions. This comparison helped determine where models needed refinement.

Results & impact

The experiments provided a concrete picture of the system's dynamic behavior and highlighted which modes were most relevant in the operating range. The identified parameters improved the accuracy of subsequent simulations and increased confidence in model-based design changes.

The measurement and analysis workflow can be reused for similar assemblies, making it easier to integrate experimental feedback into future projects.
