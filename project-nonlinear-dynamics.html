Project

Exploiting Bistability and Viscoelasticity in Reservoir Computing

Demonstrating the efficacy of nonlinear viscoelastic oscillator chain as a reservoir computer and studying the effects of viscoelasticity which introduces fading memory in the system.

Role: Lead researcher · Context: PhD research in mechanical engineering
Dynamics & vibration Numerical simulation MATLAB Electronics

Overview

Reservoir Computing utilizes the inherent nonlinear dynamics of a mechanical system to perform intelligent computations—for example time series prediction or logic operations. The success of a reservoir computer depends on its ability to address the memory-nonlinearity trade-off. In this project, we investigate the role of bistability (which introduces nonlinearity) and viscoelasticity (which introduces fading memory) in enhancing the performance of the reservoir. We show that by tuning the viscoelastic properties of the system, optimal reservoir performance can be extracted based on the task at hand.

A reservoir computer was implemented using a chain of bistable mechanical oscillators capable of large amplitude oscillations between two stable states, which introduces strong nonlinearity. The oscillators were coupled to a viscoelastic element (standard linear solid element) that introduces short-term memory. The governing equations were derived from first principles and subsequently nondimensionalized. The system was numerically simulated in MATLAB using a time-stepping scheme, and parameter ranges were identified for optimal reservoir performance using the information rate as the metric for discrete tasks and nonlinear mean square error for continuous tasks.

Methods & tools

The modeling work combined analytical and numerical techniques. I started with the equations of motion for the structure, including relevant nonlinear terms, and derived a form suitable for time-domain simulation.

I implemented the model in MATLAB, using:

  • Time integration schemes for stiff and non-stiff systems
  • Parameter sweeps to explore frequency response and stability
  • Post-processing scripts to generate response curves and phase portraits

Where appropriate, I linearized the model about operating points to compare linear and nonlinear predictions and relate results back to classical vibration theory.

My role

I led the modeling and simulation effort, including:

  • Defining the modeling scope and assumptions
  • Deriving and implementing the nonlinear equations of motion
  • Designing parameter studies and organizing simulation campaigns
  • Comparing predictions with experimental data and refining the model

I also prepared technical figures and documentation so that the workflow could be reused and extended in related studies.

Results & impact

The project highlighted the ability of tuning viscoelastic properties of a reservoir to optimize performance for different computational tasks. The memory–nonlinearity tradeoff was clearly demonstrated, showing a contrasting influence of nonlinearity and viscoelasticity on reservoir performance.

These insights informed design recommendations for the structure, such as how to adjust stiffness and mass to avoid undesirable operating regimes. More broadly, the modeling approach can be adapted to other systems with similar nonlinear characteristics.
